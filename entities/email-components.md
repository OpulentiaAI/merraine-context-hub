# Email components

Fill every part or leave it `[NEED]`. Do not invent a subject to look finished.
This is the pack for one mail, not a sequence. See [[assembling-email-components]].

# From
Jeremy Sanchez, from an address the recipient will recognise as him.
A department name or a no-reply address fails. Until Gmail is connected,
this part stays `[NEED: sending address]`.

# Subject
Written last, with the preview, after the body exists.
Front-load the real message in the first 35 to 40 characters. Specific
beats clever. Never open with `Re:` or `Fwd:` unless this mail really
is a reply or a forward.

# Preview
The second most interesting thing. Never repeats the subject. Never left
unset. An unset preview becomes whatever the client grabs first, often
"View this email in your browser".

# First line
The point. Not a warm-up. Many people never leave the preview pane.
A name, then the cited event or the change. See [[adapting-for-email]].

# Body
One job. Short paragraphs, one to three sentences. More white space than
a page. Write to one person. Links name the destination. Nothing that
matters lives only in an image.

# Action
One. Reply, or a 15-minute call, or a pass. Permission to pass stays.
A second ask moves to a later mail or gets cut.

# Sign-off
**Best, Jeremy** unless the thread is already warm, then Cheers is fine.
A team or a product name does not sign.

# Plain text
Always written by hand. Full URLs. Same headings, same one action.
An autogenerated version reads as broken markup.
