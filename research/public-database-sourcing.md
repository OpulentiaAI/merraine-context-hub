# Public-database sourcing over bought lists

## The play

Bought contact databases are saturated. The author's framing: every CEO and founder
in one gets roughly 100 cold emails a day, yours is number 98, and you are not
competing, you are being deleted. His claimed contrast is 0.5% reply from a bought
list against 5-12% from six public sources almost nobody scrapes.

| Source | What it proves |
|---|---|
| USASpending.gov | A federal contract award with a real value and date — confirmed budget |
| Crunchbase funding rounds | Capital just landed and has to be deployed |
| BuiltWith technology lookups | Their stack is a public confession of their priorities |
| LinkedIn job postings | The posting states exactly what they cannot do today |
| SEC EDGAR filings | Legally required disclosure of vendors and strategic priorities |
| G2 / Capterra reviewers | They bought something, they are comparing, and their profile is attached |

Then reference the signal directly rather than personalising: *"saw you're hiring 3
SDRs, pipeline must be top priority."* That is not flattery, it is proof you know
something.

## How we apply it for Merraine

This is the entire answer to ask #5, "a market signal tracker on raises, executive
moves, and job postings — that's the leading indicator for who's about to hire a
search firm."

For a search firm the ranking inverts slightly from the original: **job postings
outrank funding**, because a senior seat open past 30 days is a firm that has
already tried and failed to fill it internally. Funding is a strong second because
it dates the hiring plan.

Treat the author's reply-rate numbers as **his reported result**, never as ours. We
have not reproduced them and must not quote them to Jeremy as our own.
